## Prose Constraints

- Stay inside the viewpoint character's available knowledge and attention.
- Let action, selection of detail, and subtext carry emotion before explaining it.
- Prefer precise story-world details over interchangeable poetic imagery.
- Allow interruption, omission, misunderstanding, and unanswered questions.
- End scenes on an action, discovery, or decision rather than a thematic summary.
- Avoid placeholder cliches, unearned exposition, viewpoint drift, uniformly
  articulate dialogue, and decorative metaphors that do not belong to the voice.

## Review Signals

These are signals to inspect in context, not words to delete mechanically:

- action or imagery followed by a sentence explaining its emotional meaning;
- repeated balanced clauses, rhetorical triads, and similarly sized paragraphs;
- rapid insight, reconciliation, or agreement without enough dramatic friction;
- repeated hedges such as “somehow,” “seemed,” “almost,” “仿佛,” “似乎,”
  “某种,” “悄然,” “微微,” and “不由得”;
- chapter endings that summarize the lesson or announce a new beginning.
